Hyperbolic Axis Visualizer

2 ·  The axis in the upper half-plane ℍ

The red semicircle is the geodesic axis of g (the perpendicular half-circle joining its two fixed points on the real line); the arrow points toward the attracting fixed point. The blue tiles are the tessellation of ℍ by the translates gF of the modular fundamental domain F = { |z| ≥ 1, |Re z| ≤ ½ } (shaded), so you can see how the axis threads through the domains it crosses.
What is being computed?

Following Basmajian & Suzzi Valli, the modular group PSL(2,ℤ) ≅ ℤ2 ∗ ℤ3 is generated by a of order 2 and b of order 3, represented by

A = 0−110     B = 1−110.

Each syllable ab+1 and ab−1 is a parabolic; the product g is computed exactly over the integers. When |tr g| > 2 the element is hyperbolic with two real fixed points z± = &bigl;(a−d) ± √(tr²−4)&bigr; / 2c, and its axis is the half-circle on those endpoints. Runs of a single sign give parabolics (cusp excursions), so a sign change is needed for a hyperbolic axis.

3 ·  The closed geodesic on S = ℍ / PSL(2,ℤ)

In the quotient the axis descends to a closed geodesic on the modular orbifold. It is drawn by folding one period of the axis back into the fundamental domain F — repeatedly applying T: z→z±1 and S: z→−1/z until |Re z| ≤ ½ and |z| ≥ 1. The cone points i (order 2), ρ (order 3) and the cusp are marked; how high the curve climbs shows how deep g wanders into the cusp.
