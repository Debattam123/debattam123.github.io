Hecke Triangle Group Axis Visualizer

Axes of elements g = a bε1 a bε2 … a bεn of the Hecke triangle group Gp = ⟨S, Tλ⟩,  λ = 2cos(π/p), drawn in the upper half-plane together with the closed geodesic each one induces on the Hecke orbifold ℍ/Gp. The case p = 3 (λ = 1) is the modular group PSL(2, ℤ).

2 ·  The axis in the upper half-plane ℍ

drag to pan · scroll to zoom
The red semicircle is the geodesic axis of g (the half-circle meeting the real line perpendicularly at its two fixed points); the arrow points toward the attracting fixed point. The blue tiles are the tessellation of ℍ by the translates gFp of the Hecke fundamental domain Fp = { |z| ≥ 1, |Re z| ≤ λ/2 } (shaded), whose width is now set by λ, so you can see how the axis threads through the domains it crosses.
What is being computed?

The Hecke triangle group Gp is the (2, p, ∞) triangle group ≅ ℤ2 ∗ ℤp, generated by a = S of order 2 and b = STλ of order p:

A = 0−110     B = 0−11λ,  λ = 2cos(π/p).

The syllables ab and ab−1 are the two conjugate parabolics (−Tλ and its transpose), so a sign change in the exponents is needed for a hyperbolic axis, exactly as in the modular case (p = 3, λ = 1). When |tr g| > 2 the element is hyperbolic with two real fixed points z± = &bigl;(a−d) ± √(tr²−4)&bigr; / 2c, and its axis is the half-circle on those endpoints. Since λ is irrational for p ≠ 3, the matrices are computed in floating point rather than exactly.

3 ·  The closed geodesic on S = ℍ / Gp

drag to pan · scroll to zoom
In the quotient the axis descends to a closed geodesic on the Hecke orbifold. It is drawn by folding one period of the axis back into Fp — repeatedly applying Tλ: z→z±λ and S: z→−1/z until |Re z| ≤ λ/2 and |z| ≥ 1. The cone points i (order 2), ρp = eiπ/p (order p) and the cusp are marked; how high the curve climbs shows how deep g wanders into the cusp.
